Project 01 · Risk & BI

FinLend Fraud Analytics

Business problem

A synthetic African fintech was losing more to fraud each quarter but could not say where the exposure sat, so controls were being applied to everyone instead of to the risk. 250,000 transactions worth $23.87M were available and largely unexamined.

Investigation

Profiled and quality checked the raw transactions, staged and transformed them, then built a star schema in SQL Server for merchant, geography, account age, and time of day analysis. Segmented risk rather than looking at a single fraud rate, and tuned the heavy queries, achieving 85% fewer logical reads, 45% less CPU, and 46% faster elapsed time. Delivered in Power BI with DAX measures and Row Level Security.

Key findings

Fraud rose from 2.39% in Q1 to 3.32% in the final quarter, a 39% increase, totaling $917.58K in fraudulent value. The exposure was concentrated. Crypto Exchange merchants had a 10.12% fraud rate, accounts under 30 days old 6.23%, IP country mismatches 6.49% compared with 2.75% when country matched, and transactions between 12am and 5am 5.59%.

Why it matters

The rise was not general. It was concentrated in a handful of identifiable segments, so risk controls can be targeted at new accounts, crypto merchants, mismatched geographies, and overnight activity instead of adding friction across the whole customer base.

Scope note: This is an analytical case study on synthetic data, not a production fraud detection system.

Project 02 · Operations & BI

Supply Chain Delay Analysis

Business problem

More than half of deliveries were arriving late across 180,519 orders, three years, 23 regions, 4 shipping modes, and 50 product categories. But no one could say whether the cause was seasonal, regional, or structural, so there was nothing specific to fix.

Investigation

Profiled first. Delivery day fields carried the wrong data type and had to be corrected before any delay measure was trustworthy. Same Day records also revealed a data and system configuration issue that distorts delay measurement, so they were treated separately. Then tested the obvious explanations, including season, region, and category, before testing shipping mode against its promised service level.

Key findings

57.8% of deliveries were late. Regional late rates clustered tightly between 52.6% and 62.2%, and there was no meaningful seasonal pattern, which ruled out geography and seasonality as the primary drivers. First Class was late on 100% of 27,814 orders, while the cheapest option, Standard Class, was the strongest at 39.8%.

Why it matters

A 100% late rate is not necessarily an operations failure. It is a definition failure. The First Class SLA promised a transit time the carrier never achieved, making this a contractual and service definition problem rather than a routing problem. The action is to renegotiate or restate the service promise and run a weekly exception report so operations work the genuinely abnormal lanes instead of a list where everything is red.

Project 03 · Customer & Predictive Analytics

Fidelis National Bank Customer Churn Analysis

Business problem

One in five customers was leaving. The bank needed to know who was leaving, whether the leavers were worth keeping, and whether churn could be anticipated early enough to act on.

Investigation

Segmented 10,000 customers by geography, age, balance, product holdings, and activity, then fitted a logistic regression to test which factors held up together and reported the analysis in Power BI across overview, who, why, and model pages.

Key findings

Overall churn was 20.37%, concentrated in Germany at 32.4% and among customers aged 50–60 at 56.2%. Leavers held higher balances on average ($91,108 vs $72,745). Product holdings were the sharpest signal, with 82.7% churn at three products and 100% at four. Inactive members churned at 26.9% compared with 14.3% for active ones. Age was the strongest positive coefficient, while active membership was strongly protective.

Why it matters

The bank was losing its higher balance customers, not its marginal ones, and the strongest levers are reactivating dormant members and reviewing what happens when a customer is sold a third or fourth product.

Limitation, stated plainly

The model reached 81.1% accuracy but only 20% recall on churners. With roughly a fifth of customers churning, accuracy mostly rewards predicting “stays.” It missed four out of five of the customers the business actually wants to catch, so it is not fit for targeting retention spend as it stands. The segment findings should drive action. The model needs class imbalance handling and threshold tuning, evaluated on recall and precision, before anyone relies on it.
